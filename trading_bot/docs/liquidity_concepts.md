# Liquidity Concepts

## What is Liquidity?

In ICT trading, liquidity refers to clusters of stop-loss orders that institutions target to fill their large positions. Retail traders place predictable stop losses at obvious levels, creating pools of orders that smart money exploits.

## Types of Liquidity

### Buy-Side Liquidity (BSL)
Stop-loss orders **above** swing highs:
- From traders short with stops above highs
- From breakout buy stops
- Institutions raid these to sell/short

```
BSL ────────────●  Stop losses above highs
            ┌──┤
            │  │ Swing High
        ────┼──┘
            │
```

### Sell-Side Liquidity (SSL)
Stop-loss orders **below** swing lows:
- From traders long with stops below lows
- From breakdown sell stops
- Institutions raid these to buy/long

```
            │
        ────┼──┐
            │  │ Swing Low
            └──┤
SSL ────────────●  Stop losses below lows
```

### Equal Highs (EQH)
Two or more swing highs at similar price:
- Creates obvious "resistance"
- Retail shorts place stops just above
- Massive liquidity pool

```
        ┌──┤  ┌──┤
        │  │  │  │  Equal Highs
        │  │  │  │
──●─────┴──┴──┴──┴────── EQH level
  ↑ BSL pool
```

### Equal Lows (EQL)
Two or more swing lows at similar price:
- Creates obvious "support"
- Retail longs place stops just below
- Massive liquidity pool

```
  ↓ SSL pool
──●─────┬──┬──┬──┬────── EQL level
        │  │  │  │
        │  │  │  │  Equal Lows
        └──┤  └──┤
```

## Liquidity Concepts

### Liquidity Sweep
When price briefly breaks through a liquidity level:
- Triggers stop-loss orders
- Fills institutional positions
- Price quickly reverses

### Liquidity Grab
Same as sweep - taking out stops:
- Aggressive move through level
- "Grabbing" the liquidity
- Often creates reversal candle

### Stop Hunt
Intentional move to trigger stops:
- Price breaks obvious level
- Stops are triggered
- Price reverses
- Retail gets stopped out, institutions profit

### Inducement
Creating obvious liquidity to trap traders:
- Market creates "easy" setup
- Traders enter with obvious stops
- Price sweeps the stops
- Real move begins opposite direction

## Liquidity Runs

### Buy-Side Liquidity Run
When price is targeting BSL above:
- Market is moving up
- Targeting stops above swing highs
- Once BSL is taken, watch for reversal

### Sell-Side Liquidity Run
When price is targeting SSL below:
- Market is moving down
- Targeting stops below swing lows
- Once SSL is taken, watch for reversal

## Trading Liquidity

### Strategy 1: Trade After Sweep
1. Identify obvious liquidity (EQH/EQL, swing points)
2. Wait for price to sweep the liquidity
3. Look for reversal signal
4. Enter in opposite direction of sweep
5. Target opposing liquidity

### Strategy 2: Trade Toward Liquidity
1. Identify trend direction
2. Find liquidity in trend direction
3. Enter on pullback (OB/FVG)
4. Target the liquidity pool
5. Trail stop or exit at liquidity

### Entry After Liquidity Sweep

**Bullish Setup** (after SSL sweep):
1. Price sweeps below swing low(s)
2. Creates reversal candle/pattern
3. Bullish CHoCH or MSS on LTF
4. Enter long with stop below sweep low
5. Target: BSL above

**Bearish Setup** (after BSL sweep):
1. Price sweeps above swing high(s)
2. Creates reversal candle/pattern
3. Bearish CHoCH or MSS on LTF
4. Enter short with stop above sweep high
5. Target: SSL below

## Liquidity + Other Concepts

### Liquidity + Order Block
- Price sweeps liquidity
- Returns to OB that formed on sweep
- High probability reversal zone

### Liquidity + FVG
- Sweep creates FVG
- Price returns to fill FVG
- Entry at FVG after sweep

### Liquidity + Structure
- Sweep creates structure break
- CHoCH or MSS after sweep
- Confirms reversal

## Session-Based Liquidity

### Asian Session
- Often creates liquidity pools
- Range highs and lows become targets
- London/NY sweeps these levels

### London Session
- Often sweeps Asian liquidity
- Creates new highs/lows
- Real move after sweep

### New York Session
- May continue London direction
- Or sweep London levels for reversal
- Highest volume = biggest moves

## Practical Tips

### Identifying High-Probability Liquidity

1. **Multiple touches** = More stops
   - Double/triple tops and bottoms
   - Equal highs/lows
   
2. **Recent levels** = Fresh stops
   - Recent swing points
   - Today's high/low
   - Session extremes

3. **Obvious levels** = More retail stops
   - Round numbers
   - Previous day high/low
   - Major swing points

### Avoid Getting Swept

1. Don't place stops at obvious levels
2. Use structure-based stops (beyond OB)
3. Give stops room to breathe
4. Consider hidden stop strategies

### Using Liquidity for Targets

1. Identify nearest liquidity in trade direction
2. Use as take-profit target
3. Price often reaches these levels
4. Consider partial profits before

## Key Rules

1. **Liquidity is the magnet**
   - Price seeks liquidity
   - Don't fight it

2. **Wait for the sweep**
   - Best entries after liquidity is taken
   - Patience pays

3. **EQH/EQL = High priority**
   - Double/triple tops/bottoms
   - Almost always get swept

4. **Session matters**
   - Asian creates liquidity
   - London/NY sweeps it

5. **Combine with structure**
   - Sweep + CHoCH = High probability
   - Sweep + MSS = Very high probability

## Common Mistakes

1. Entering before liquidity sweep
2. Placing stops at obvious liquidity
3. Ignoring session context
4. Fighting liquidity runs
5. Not waiting for confirmation after sweep
