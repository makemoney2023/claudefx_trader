# Swing Exhaustion Validation Framework

## Overview

This document describes a **supplementary validation framework** that enhances ICT entry criteria. It does NOT replace the core ICT methodology (market structure, FVGs, order blocks, liquidity sweeps). Instead, it provides additional confluence by ensuring price action has "matured" before committing to an entry.

These concepts are fractal and universal -- they apply to every pair, every market, and every timeframe.

---

## The 4-6 Swing Rule

### Core Principle

Before expecting a reversal at a Point of Interest (POI), count the structural swings leading into the zone. You should observe **at least 4 to 6 swings** (e.g., 4 higher highs and 4 higher lows trending into supply, or 4 lower lows and 4 lower highs trending into demand) before the move is considered "mature" enough to reverse.

### Why It Matters

- **Insufficient swings (1-2):** Price is likely in an impulsive move that will smash straight through the zone rather than reversing. Entering here is "catching a falling knife."
- **3 swings:** The move may be developing but is not yet exhausted. Higher risk of continuation through the zone.
- **4-6 swings:** The trend is typically exhausting itself. After the 4th swing, price often enters a brief consolidation phase before shifting direction.
- **More than 6 swings:** The move may be overextended. Still valid for reversal but watch for false breaks.

### How to Count

1. Drop to a lower timeframe relative to the POI (e.g., if the POI is on the 15-minute, count swings on the 1-minute or 5-minute).
2. Identify each structural swing high and swing low as price trends into the zone.
3. Each pair (swing high + swing low) counts as one swing.
4. The count starts from the most recent break of structure that initiated the move toward the POI.

### Application

- **Counter-trend entries:** When price approaches a HTF supply/demand zone, count the swings on the LTF. Need 4+ swings before considering a reversal trade.
- **Pro-trend pullbacks:** When the trend is up and price pulls back to a demand zone, the pullback itself should show 4-6 swings on the LTF before expecting the bounce.
- **Confidence adjustment:** If swings < 4, reduce confidence. If swings >= 4 with other confluence, increase confidence.

## Rounding and Consolidation

### After the 4th Swing

Once the swing count reaches 4-6, do NOT enter immediately. Look for a change in price behavior:

- **Consolidation:** Price enters a small range or box after the 4th swing. This indicates momentum is fading.
- **Rounding pattern:** A semicircle or inverse cup shape at the top/bottom of the move. This shows institutional participation shifting direction.
- **Momentum divergence:** The later swings become smaller, showing decreasing momentum.

### What Rounding Looks Like

- At supply (bearish reversal): Price forms a dome/rounded top, with each push higher being weaker.
- At demand (bullish reversal): Price forms a rounded bottom/saucer, with each push lower being weaker.
- The "floor" or "ceiling" of the rounding pattern is a key level to watch for the break.

## The Sweep (Entry Trigger)

### No Sweep = You Are the Liquidity

Even with 4-6 swings and rounding, you need a **catalyst** before entering:

1. **The Sweep:** Price must sweep a high or low (liquidity pool) before reversing. This is the market maker collecting stops and limit orders.
2. **Break of Structure:** After the sweep, wait for price to break structure in the opposite direction.
3. **Entry:** Take the entry on the **retracement (30-70%)** back into the range created by the break of structure.

### If There Is No Sweep

If price hits your zone without sweeping liquidity first, your position likely becomes the liquidity that gets swept. Wait for the sweep before committing.

## Prominent Wick Identification

### What Is a Prominent Wick?

A prominent wick is a specific liquidity point that acts as a magnet for price manipulation before a reversal or continuation. It represents a pool of liquidity the market maker needs to access.

### How to Identify

1. **The Zoom Test:** The wick must be visually obvious. You should be able to zoom in AND zoom out of the chart and still see it clearly. If you have to squint or search for it, it is not prominent enough.
2. **Standalone:** The wick stands out from surrounding price action -- it is not part of a cluster of similar wicks.
3. **Location:** Found within a previous range (consolidation) that caused a break of structure.

### Trading the Prominent Wick

1. Price forms a rounding pattern that builds liquidity.
2. Price breaks the "floor" of the rounding pattern.
3. Price drops to tap the prominent wick located to the left.
4. Look for an **immediate push** back into the previous range after the wick is swept.
5. This reaction confirms the wick was the intended liquidity target.

### As Entry and Target

- **Entry:** Wait for the sweep and reclaim of the prominent wick, then enter on the retracement.
- **Target:** The beginning (base) of a prominent wick can be used as a take-profit target, as price often retraces immediately upon reaching it.

## Avoid the First Hour

The first hour of a major session open (especially New York) often features manipulation:

- Price spikes in one direction to trap traders.
- Then reverses to sweep the opposite side.
- Wait for the **second swing** -- where price breaks out, establishes a structure level, and confirms the real direction.
- This applies especially to less experienced setups. The second swing provides much better risk-to-reward.

## Trailing Stop Using 21 EMA

An alternative trailing method for managing positions:

1. Once in profit, overlay a 21 EMA on the execution timeframe.
2. If price closes below the 21 EMA and then pushes back above, place your stop behind the new low.
3. Continue trailing behind swing lows (in an uptrend) or impulsive candle lows.
4. This keeps you in the trade during normal retracements while protecting against true reversals.

## Validation Checklist

Before entering any trade, validate with this supplementary checklist:

- [ ] **Step 1:** Is price entering a valid Higher Timeframe POI (supply/demand zone, rejection block, wick)?
- [ ] **Step 2:** Has the move INTO the POI created at least 4-6 distinct swings on the lower timeframe?
- [ ] **Step 3:** Is there consolidation, rounding, or momentum divergence after the swings?
- [ ] **Step 4:** Has price swept a high/low (liquidity) and then broken structure in the reversal direction?

If all four steps are met alongside standard ICT confluence (FVG, OB, liquidity sweep, HTF alignment), the setup has high probability.

If Step 2 is not met (fewer than 4 swings), reduce confidence significantly -- the move may not be exhausted enough to reverse.
